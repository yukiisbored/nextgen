# Budget GabeCube (Steam Machine)

I recently moved to a new apartment.
Before this, I was living in shared homes (WGs) or shitty studio apartments.

It's the first proper apartment that is my own.

As I'll have my own proper living room, I immediately splurged on a nice
television as I'm absolutely tired of living on the same desk for hours every
day.

I gotta say, it's absolutely nice being able to have physical separation
between life and work.

But how about gaming?
I've always been a PC gamer, but it's not out of choice.
It's circumstances.

## "Not available in your country"

Growing up in Indonesia, piracy was the norm.
It's as simple as that.
It's not because we had a choice, really.
No one wanted to serve the Indonesian market, so pirates filled the scene.

Then Steam started officially supporting Indonesian users by using our currency
and payment methods.

Keep in mind, in Indonesia, when I grew up, having a bank account is not a
given.
Even if they do, their bank card won't be a Mastercard/Visa, let alone support
online transactions.

The norm was to go to an ATM to do a wire transfer and merchants leverage the
instant transfer times enabled by our national payment networks.
Banks even provide "virtual accounts" for one time payments or provide a
dedicated option in their ATMs to pay bills to local businesses.

## PC Gaming

Because was Steam one of the pioneers of supporting the Indonesian market, I have a pretty sizable Steam library.

It's hard for me to justify becoming a console gamer because that means I have
to rebuy my games.
Even then, if I buy them console games digitally, it's not guaranteed that I
could still play them.

While in theory this could happen with Steam, things are always well enough
that I don't worry about it.

At the same time though, I always envy the pick up and play experience of
consoles.
I don't like the experience of playing games on the computer because it feels
like operating heavy machinery.

Aside from the PC, I also grew up with the Wii and Xbox 360.
It just feels more fun and relaxing being laid back on the sofa and play games
on a controller.
Well, the Wii is kinda stretching it, but lets ignore that.

## Enter SteamOS

Valve invested heavily in SteamOS to make PC gaming accessible and honestly,
it's fucking great.

I kept trying to get the original Steam Big Picture working, but it always
felt clunky.
The new Steam Big Picture mode introduced by the Steam Deck completely changed
all of that.

It's honestly so good that I started buying games from Steam over GOG, just to
get a smooth integrated experience.

## Steam Machine

I remember talking to my partner last year about getting a Steam Machine as one
of the first purchases for the new apartment.
I knew it was going to be more expensive than an Xbox or a PS5, but I'm
tied to the ecosystem due to my games collection.

Unfortunately, the computer hardware armageddon is happening now.

I built my gaming PC for around 1000 EUR.
Now the 4 TB SSD alone costs that much.
I don't even want to think about the cost of the 32 GB RAM kit.

The Steam Machine costs as much as my gaming PC originally, but with much worse
specs.

Honestly, fuck that.
But SteamOS is just out there, available to download on install.

So, I decided to just turn my PC into a GabeCube by installing SteamOS on it.

And yes, it's the same SteamOS on the Steam Deck, Lenovo Legion Go, and other
official SteamOS devices.
I didn't choose Bazzite or other clones, because I tried it before and I didn't
like it.
Personally, I dislike "enthusiast" Linux distros.

I installed SteamOS and because it's an all AMD build, everything works
perfectly out of the box.

## Human interface

I have a rather sizable collection of controllers.
It's a bit of an obsession, honestly.

I wanted to get a Steam Contoller, but with that price and the expected shipping
window. Yeah, no.

I bought a PCIe WiFi card from MediaMarkt and unfortunately, it was one of those
MediaTek 7925 chipsets.
But considering that it's been somewhat standard on laptops, surely, the
support is good now, right?

Yeah, no.
At least the WiFi part works fine, but the Bluetooth was abysmal.
It pissed me off, honestly.

I started looking for alternatives.
Me and my partner never had good luck with using Bluetooth for controllers.
It's been a curse, honestly.

Luckily, I'm a weirdo who owns a Mayflash adapter, because I main Tetris with
the Joycons.
It works, but having to resync is annoying.

I started looking online for solutions and it's hard.
Most search results just ends up with "how to connect controller via Bluetooth".
There's not much discussion about "non-Bluetooth" solutions.

I was looking into the OpenPuck project which is an open hardware/source
implementation of the Steam Controller's puck.

From there, I stumbled upon this project, which I believe is the best Steam
Controller alternative for the DIY GabeCube.

https://github.com/kungaa/DS5-Linux-Bridge

It's a DIY adapter for the PS5 controller which behaves as if it's connected
over USB.
That means the fancy features such as HD haptics and wireless audio works.
Plus, it can even pretend to be a keyboard to enable waking the PC sleep with
the controller.

And yeah, the Steam Input supports the PS5 controller perfectly.
Hell, the built-in Linux drivers works great too.
Desktop mode works great once you've activated it.
My beloved gyro works.
And my god, the Touchpad is a godsend.
Ignoring the stupid position of it though.

It is the perfect non-Steam Controller Steam Controller for non-Steam Machine
Steam Machines.

So yeah, I'm very happy with my DIY GabeCube setup.
It works really great.
The only thing I'm annoyed with is the blinking power light when it's sleeping.
